# Rock photo download manifest — L24 Wikimedia images

These four photographs are referenced in `lectures/24_rock_magnetism.md` but
cannot be downloaded inside the build sandbox (no network access to
`commons.wikimedia.org`). To complete the L24 figure set, download them
once into `assets/figures/` from the URLs below.

Each photo carries its own license. The accompanying `.LICENSE.txt` files
must travel with the image — these are part of the textbook's licensing
compliance (see the §2 note in the L24 lecture file about CC BY-SA 3.0
mixed-license images embedded in a CC BY 4.0 work).

## License compatibility note (important — read once)

Three of the four photos (magnetite, hematite, pyrrhotite) are **CC BY-SA 3.0**.
The rest of the textbook is under **CC BY 4.0**. Two-license mixing in
educational works is well-precedented and legally clean as long as **each
photo carries its own license attribution in the caption + a sidecar
`.LICENSE.txt`**, which is what's set up above.

The textbook's own license terms are not affected; only the photo retains
its CC BY-SA 3.0 license. If you'd rather avoid mixed licensing entirely,
the alternatives (in decreasing order of effort) are:

1. **Use only CC BY images / public-domain photos.** USGS Photo Library
   has acceptable specimens (e.g. <https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2005/1198/>),
   though the photos are smaller and less polished. James St. John's
   Flickr collection (CC BY 2.0) is another option.
2. **Generate AI photographic-style images.** Acceptable for non-data
   figures; not advised for a textbook that values empirical truth.
3. **Take your own photos.** UW Earth & Space Sciences hand-sample
   collection presumably has all four minerals.

For the Phase 2 hand-off the CC BY-SA 3.0 path is the lowest-friction.
