# L27 — Master Rifting Continuum Matrix
## Instructor-private reference for the Round 2 reveal

This is the canonical version of the rifting continuum spectrum that anchors the second predict-then-reveal round in L27 §5.2. Use this matrix as the reveal artifact. Reveal each row one-at-a-time as students share their predictions; do not show the full table until every row has been discussed.

Six stages × six attributes. The columns deliberately mix sign-of-anomaly (Bouguer), absolute value (heat flow), qualitative state (topography, magma), dimensionless ratio (β), and behavioural type (seismicity) — students should leave knowing that "the geophysical signature" of a rift is *a vector*, not a single number.

## Notes for the reveal

The matrix is **not memorisable**. The point is *patterns*:

- **Bouguer** is negative across the entire active part of the continuum. The amplitude grows from 1 to 3 (more magmatic root, more thinned crust) then becomes more diffuse at 5–6 (root spread out across the whole ridge zone). At stage 4, the central positive over a magmatic high is a new feature that arises only when the rift has organized into segments with concentrated melt.
- **Heat flow** is monotonically increasing 1 → 6, modulated by the $1/\sqrt{t}$ decay across mature oceanic crust at stage 5–6.
- **Topographic expression** flips sign in the middle of the continuum: surface elevation peaks at stages 2–3 (uplift), bottoms out as the rift breaks through (stage 4), then becomes negative (below sea level) at stages 5–6 (oceanic depths).
- **β** rises monotonically and asymptotes once the continental lithosphere is fully replaced.
- **Magmatism** evolves from nothing → alkaline → bimodal → dyke-dominated → episodic → continuous. The pivot from continental to MOR-style is at the rift-to-spread transition.
- **Seismicity** stays brittle and shallow through stages 2–4, then transitions to combined brittle (axial fault) + magmatic (dyke) sources at stages 5–6.

## Suggested pedagogical move during reveal

For each row in turn:

1. Ask: "What did your group predict for Bouguer at incipient rifting?" — let two or three groups answer aloud.
2. Reveal the row. Connect to the lecture's prior physics if possible.
3. Move to the next row.

Doing the matrix one row at a time keeps the cognitive load manageable. Doing it column-by-column (e.g. "Bouguer in all six stages") works equally well and emphasises the trend in one observable, which some classes find clearer.

## Common student misconceptions to look for

1. **"Continental rifts are smaller versions of mid-ocean ridges."** — False. The dynamics (β, magmatism, gravity) are qualitatively different in stages 2–3 vs. 5–6. The continuum *connects* them but they are not similar.
2. **"Bouguer over an ocean ridge should be positive because the ridge is high."** — A common error of intuition; the Bouguer correction *removes* the topographic signal. The remaining negative signal is the mantle root. This is exactly what L21 (isostasy) prepared them for, but it often needs to be re-emphasised here.
3. **"Failed rifts (Keweenawan) are 'pre-stage-1'."** — No. Failed rifts have a *different* signature (gabbroic underplating → positive central Bouguer) than stable craton (~zero). The matrix above stops at stage 6 of the *active* continuum; failed rifts are a separate branch best illustrated in F10c.
