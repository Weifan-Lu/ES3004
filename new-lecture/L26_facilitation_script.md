# L26 Instructor-Private: Facilitation Script for the Three Beats

A short pacing-and-discussion guide for the three predict-then-reveal beats in §6 of the lecture. The whole comparison matrix takes roughly **40 minutes** of class time if you do it properly. Don't try to compress this.

---

## Setup (3 min)

Before any beat:

- Hand out (or have students open) a blank comparison-matrix worksheet with the 11 attribute rows pre-labeled and two empty columns for oceanic / continental.
- Explicitly tell the class: *"You will predict each row before I show you the answer. The point is to surface what you already think you know."*
- The worksheet should fit on **one page** so students can see the whole framework at a glance.

---

## Beat A — Composition, density, subduction (10 min)

**Predict phase (5 min).** Read the four prompts aloud:

1. What is the dominant rock type of oceanic crust? Of continental crust?
2. Which lithosphere is denser?
3. Which subducts?
4. Sketch a density profile for each.

Walk around. Look at sketches.

**Discussion phase (3 min).** Before revealing, ask: *"Who predicted that continental mantle is heavier than oceanic mantle? Hands up."* Some hands will go up. Then: *"Who predicted the opposite?"* This is the productive disagreement. Don't resolve it yet.

**Reveal phase (2 min).** Show F7 (oceanic Vp) and F9 (continental Vp). Walk through composition → density. **Then drop the punchline:**

> *Cratonic mantle is chemically depleted. It is colder than oceanic mantle lithosphere — and it is also LESS dense. That is why cratons are stable for billions of years.*

This usually produces visible "huh!" reactions. Hold the moment.

---

## Beat B — Thickness, seismic structure, "what is the lithosphere?" (15 min)

This is the longest beat. It is the heart of the lecture.

**Predict phase (5 min).** Read the three prompts:

1. Predict $V_p(z)$, Moho depth, total lithospheric thickness for 100 Ma Pacific.
2. Same, for the Canadian Shield.
3. Same, for the East African Rift.

Walk around. Look at sketches.

**Reveal phase (4 min).** Show F11 (continental vs oceanic $V_p$). Walk through the comparison:

- Oceanic Moho is sharp and shallow (~11 km from sea surface). Continental Moho is deeper and gradational.
- Oceanic LVZ at ~60 km is clear. Continental LVZ is weak or absent under cratons.

**The pivot (6 min) — this is where the lecture earns its tuition.** Show F6 (the boundary-layers key figure). Walk through panel (a) first — oceanic boundary layers thicken with √age, both definitions track each other. Then panel (b).

Pause. Point at the four horizontal lines in panel (b). Say:

> *Every one of these is a "lithosphere base." They are all measurements of the same craton. They are 80, 150, 220, and 280 kilometers. They should agree. They don't.*

Wait.

Then ask: *"Why do they agree under oceans?"* Take 2-3 answers. The right answer is some version of: oceanic lithosphere is young, thermally simple, and doesn't have a chemical-buoyancy layer at the bottom to complicate things. Cratons have had billions of years to develop independent thermal, chemical, mechanical, and seismic structure, and the four properties have decoupled.

This is the lecture's thesis. Make sure every student has it written down before you move on.

---

## Beat C — Heat flow, gravity, magnetics, age (10 min)

This beat is faster because students are now in the comparison mindset.

**Predict phase (3 min).** Read all six prompts at once. Students predict on their worksheets.

**Reveal phase (5 min).** Speed-walk through the answers:

- Heat flow at 100 Ma Pacific: ~50 mW/m² (HSC prediction).
- Heat flow on the Canadian Shield: ~30–45 mW/m². *Why is this lower?* — pause for the answer (low radiogenic, depleted mantle).
- Bouguer over ocean basin: strongly positive. Bouguer over Himalayas: strongly negative.
- JdF: Vine–Matthews stripes. NA craton: terrane-mapped basement structure.

**The wrap (2 min).** Display the full matrix. Read aloud:

> *Oceanic lithosphere is globally homogeneous and young. Continental lithosphere is heterogeneous and preserves Earth history.*

Then say: *"The geodynamic role of each — row 11 — follows mechanically from rows 1 through 10. We will see this play out in L27, L28, L29, and L30."* This is the bridge to the rest of Module 7.

---

## Pacing checks

If you are *behind schedule* mid-lecture:

- **Skip Beat A's discussion phase**, go straight to the reveal. The chemical-buoyancy punchline still lands.
- **Do NOT skip Beat B's pivot.** That is the lecture. If you must cut, cut Beat C's wrap.
- **Beat C can be compressed to 6 minutes** if needed — students will recover the content from the matrix in their notes.

If you are *ahead of schedule*:

- Add a Beat-A side discussion: *"What does the cratonic chemical depletion tell us about Earth's early differentiation?"*
- Add a Beat-B side discussion: *"If the cratonic LAB is a gradient, can we still talk about plates as rigid?"* — this is a Module 7 capstone preview.
- The Siletzia §9 anchor naturally absorbs any remaining time.

---

## Common student misconceptions to watch for

1. **"Continental crust is more rigid than oceanic crust because it's thicker."** Both crusts are mostly elastic on seismic timescales. The rigidity-relevant property is the *temperature and rheology of the mantle lithosphere below*, not the crust above. Address this in Beat B.

2. **"The seismic LAB is the real LAB."** This is the most common error. Address it directly when showing F6: there is no single "real" LAB; there are different measurements of different aspects of the lithosphere-asthenosphere transition.

3. **"Subduction happens because oceanic crust is heavier than continental crust."** Crust density alone is not the right comparison; the mantle lithosphere below the crust matters, and oceanic mantle lithosphere becomes denser than asthenosphere only after it has cooled for ~10 Ma. Address in Beat A.

4. **"All continental crust is Precambrian."** No — only the cratonic cores are. The continental margins (Cordillera, Andes, Cascadia) are Phanerozoic and very young. Address in Beat C row 10.
