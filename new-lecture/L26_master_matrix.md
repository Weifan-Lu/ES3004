# L26 Instructor-Private: Master Comparison Matrix

**Purpose.** This is the completed answer key for the eleven-attribute comparison matrix that students fill in during the three active-learning beats of §6. Use this as the gold-standard reference for the reveal slides and for grading.

**How to use this in class.** Display each row only *after* students have predicted it. The whole point of the matrix is that students *predict* before they *receive* each row — survey content delivered through prediction, not transcription.

| 9 | **Magnetic signature** | Spectacular Vine–Matthews–Morley striped pattern, sub-parallel to ridge, recording polarity reversals. ## Beat-by-Beat Reveal Order

Each beat covers a *theme* of the matrix, not arbitrary chunks. Reveal each row only after students have predicted it.

### Beat A — Composition, density, subduction (rows 1–3)
- Goal: build the intuition that subduction is *thermal + chemical*, not just thermal.
- Critical insight to draw out: cratonic mantle lithosphere is *chemically buoyant* despite being colder. This is the answer to "why are cratons stable for billions of years?"

### Beat B — Thickness, seismic structure, "what is the lithosphere?" (rows 4–6)
- Goal: students confront the disagreement between four different definitions of the cratonic LAB.
- Show F11 (continental vs. oceanic $V_p$) first, then F6 (boundary layers).
- This is the lecture's central thesis. Spend the most time here.

### Beat C — Heat flow, gravity, magnetics, age, geodynamic role (rows 7–11)
- Goal: students see that each method we have studied earlier in the course tells a *different story* about the same lithosphere, and the stories are consistent.
- Pull from L08 (refraction), L19–L21 (gravity), L23–L25 (magnetics).

## Why the matrix is not just a survey

If you only look at the matrix as a static reference (this document), it *is* survey content. The pedagogical move is that students *predict* each row before reading it. The predictions force the cognitive frame: "what would I expect, and why?" rather than "what does the textbook say?"

Two things to facilitate during class:

1. **Wait time.** The five minutes for the predict phase is real. Don't shorten it. Students who write down a wrong prediction learn from the reveal in a way that students who passively read the right answer do not.
2. **Listen for disagreements between students.** The richest discussions arise when two students predict opposite signs (e.g., one predicts Bouguer gravity is *positive* over the Himalayas because of the mass of the mountains; the other predicts *negative* because of the root). Use these moments. The "wrong" intuition is usually a great teaching opportunity.

The matrix earns the survey content; if you skip the prediction step, the lecture collapses back into a list of facts.
